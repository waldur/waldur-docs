    Applicant guide

    This guide follows an applicant (a principal investigator or proposal submitter) end to end: finding an open call, creating and filling in a proposal, answering the call's compliance checklist, submitting, tracking the proposal through the evaluation workflow, reading technical-assessment feedback when the call shares it, responding to the reviews and reading released reports on calls that use them, and finally responding to an award.

    For the call manager's side of the same workflow see Running the evaluation; for the reviewer's side see Reviewing proposals. What calls and proposals are is summarised in the overview.

    Note

    Screenshots in this guide use the 2025 Spring HPC Allocation Call demo call, which shares its technical assessment with applicants and includes an award-response step; those of the response to reviews and the released report use a call created from the Extreme Scale template. Your calls will show different names, offerings, checklists and reviewers, and the steps you see depend on how the call manager configured the call.

    Service access modes

    How applicants reach calls depends on the deployment's service access mode. In a marketplace-only deployment they apply from an offering and see access requests instead of proposals — see How you reach services.

    Finding an open call

    Open Calls → Calls for proposals from the sidebar. Once you have proposals, the page opens with summary cards of your own work — In draft, In review, Awarded and the Next draft deadline — each linking to the matching proposals. Below them, three tabs list the Open calls you can apply to, All calls, and the Available offerings the calls grant access to. Each call card shows the managing organisation, a short description and the submission cutoff.

    Use Details to read a call's full description and eligibility rules, or the All calls tab to browse the complete catalogue. When you have found the call you want, click Apply to call.

    The call's details page offers the same with Apply to round.

    Tip

    The Calls for proposals catalogue is also reachable without signing in, from the public landing page — handy for sharing a call with colleagues.

    A call marked This call restricts who may apply checks who you are before you can apply; when you do not qualify, Apply to call is disabled and the call's page says You are not eligible to apply to this call.

    How many proposals you may have at once

    A call-managing organization may limit how many proposals one applicant holds with it at a time, across all of its calls. A proposal you created counts while it is Submitted or In review, and once Accepted for as long as its project runs. Drafts, rejected and cancelled proposals do not count, nor do proposals on which you are only a team member.

    At the limit you cannot start or submit another proposal to that organization's calls: you see You have reached the maximum of N active projects or pending proposals. You can apply again once one of them is decided against, withdrawn, or its project has ended. A draft you have already started stays a draft; submit it when a place frees up.

    Starting a proposal

    Apply opens the Create proposal dialog. It confirms the call name, the round you are applying to and the round deadline. Give your proposal a Name and click Create.

    The proposal opens in the Applicant editor. The Progress panel on the right tracks each section's completion and holds the Submit and Save as draft actions; the main area presents the sections as expandable cards. A proposal stays in Draft — and is never seen by the call team — until you submit it, so you can save and return as often as you like.

    The editor opens on Details overview, a read-only summary of the call, round and deadline you are applying under. The editable sections follow.

    Project details

    Expand Project details and describe your project:

    Field Notes
    Name Required
    Summary Required
    Description Optional, longer free text
    Science domain Optional; pick a domain, then a sub-domain from the taxonomy your deployment maintains
    Project duration A choice from the lengths the call offers, when it offers any. Otherwise read-only: the longest subscription you request under Resource requests, or the duration the call fixes; see below
    Upload supporting documentation Optional attachments

    Required fields are marked with a red asterisk, and the card header shows how many fields are filled. Project details is expanded when the page opens, so this is the first section you see.

    Which of these fields you see, and which you must fill in, is set per call. Name is always required; each of the others may be required, optional, or not asked for at all. A call that does not ask for a field simply does not show it, so your form may be shorter than the table above. See Project details fields for the call manager's side of it.

    Project duration

    When the call offers a list of lengths, choose one under Project duration; no subscription you request may run longer, and the award may move the project to another length from the list. Otherwise you are not asked how long the project should run. When the call sets a fixed duration, the project lasts exactly that long, and any subscription length you choose must fit inside it. Otherwise, when a resource you request is sold by the month, you choose the subscription length on that request, and the project lasts as long as the longest subscription. When the call sets no fixed duration and nothing you request is sold by the month, the project has no end date. The Project details card and the Summary panel state the length this comes to.

    Resource requests

    Expand Resource requests to choose what you need. On a template-based call you request resources only through the call manager's resource templates; each fixes its offering, plan, amounts and attributes. Depending on the call:

    • Several templates — tick the ones your project requires (here, the GPU Compute Package) and click Save.
    • One template — choose one with its radio button and click Save. Choosing another replaces it. The proposal cannot be submitted without one.

    A proposal started from an offering page already requests that offering's template, unless several templates use it; then choose one here.

    Requests that are not from a template

    A request whose template was deleted, or that was not made from one, is flagged above the templates. Choose a template and click Save to replace it; until then the proposal cannot be submitted.

    Purchase orders

    When an offering requires a purchase order, supply its reference or document on the resource request. The request can be saved without one, but the proposal cannot be submitted until it is there — see Purchase orders in calls.

    Compliance checklist

    When the call has a compliance checklist, a Compliance checklist step appears between project details and resource requests. Answer every required question; the card header tracks your progress as n/N answered, and its subtitle warns that additional questions may appear based on your answers — so the total can grow as you answer.

    Checklists support many question types — single- and multi-select, numbers, dates, file uploads, confirmations and more (see Checklists and forms). Some questions are conditional: answering one may reveal a follow-up, flagged with a Shown based on: … hint naming the answer that triggered it.

    Project team

    Expand Project team to add colleagues who should have access to the resulting project and set their roles. You are added automatically as the creator.

    Reviewing and submitting

    Before submitting, review each section. The Submit button stays disabled until every required step is complete — the Progress panel shows a green tick against finished sections and an empty circle against those still needing input.

    When everything is complete, click Submit and confirm. Only a proposal manager of the team can submit; other team members see Only a proposal manager can submit the proposal. instead of the button. A note beneath the button records that submitting means agreeing to the platform's terms of service and privacy policy. The proposal moves out of Draft and into the evaluation workflow, and the call team can now see it.

    Warning

    Submit before the round deadline. Once the round closes you can no longer submit, and a proposal left in Draft is not evaluated by that round. On most calls it is not lost either: it moves into the call's next round and stays editable, and you are emailed the new deadline. A draft left at the last round's cut-off, or on a call that has carry-over turned off, is cancelled — see Drafts at the cut-off.

    Waiting for the round cut-off

    Some calls evaluate all proposals of a round together. On such a call your proposal stays Submitted after you submit it, and nothing happens to it until the round's cutoff date has passed. Evaluation then starts for every proposal of the round at once, and yours moves to In review, usually within an hour of the cut-off. Until then the proposal's stepper shows Submitted — evaluation starts after the round cut-off. under Submission, with no later step highlighted. Waiting as Submitted is expected, not a sign that something is wrong.

    Your proposals

    Open Calls → My proposals to see everything you have created, across all calls, with its call, round deadline and current state. Use the state filter to focus on drafts, submitted, in-review or accepted proposals.

    Proposal states an applicant sees:

    State Meaning
    Draft Not yet submitted — only you can see it
    Submitted Submitted and awaiting evaluation — on calls that evaluate at the round cut-off, until the cut-off has passed
    In review Moving through the evaluation workflow — on calls that announce results together for the whole round, until the round's results are published
    Accepted Approved — a project and resources have been (or are being) provisioned
    Rejected Not funded
    Canceled Withdrawn, or an award you declined

    Withdrawing a proposal

    To take a submitted proposal out of evaluation, a proposal manager of its team uses Withdraw proposal on it and may give a reason. The confirmation warns that A withdrawn proposal cannot be resubmitted. This works while the proposal is Submitted or In review; it moves to Canceled, and the call managers are told. Once an award awaits your answer, decline the award instead. A proposal still in Draft is not withdrawn but deleted, with Delete draft.

    Tracking progress

    Open a submitted proposal to follow it through the call's evaluation workflow. The stepper at the top shows every step, with completed steps ticked, the current step highlighted, and later steps greyed out. Here the proposal has cleared Administrative check and Technical assessment and is in Expert review.

    The sections below the stepper are read-only once submitted, so you always see exactly what you sent. Editing a submitted proposal is refused, not just hidden: your requested resources, documents and checklist answers are fixed at the moment you submit, and only the call team can change them afterwards — unless they ask you to (see When changes are requested). A draft you leave open when the round closes is not affected — it moves into the call's next round and stays editable, because it was never submitted (see Drafts at the cut-off).

    When changes are requested

    On some calls an evaluation step can send your proposal back to you for changes instead of rejecting it. You are notified, and the proposal shows Changes requested at with the message, the parts you may edit and, when set, the date to submit the changes by. The proposal stays In review.

    Only the parts listed become editable — for example the summary, supporting documentation or resource requests. Make the changes and click Submit changes in the Progress panel; the step then continues with what you changed. If the due date passes first, the request lapses and the step continues with the proposal as it stands.

    Which steps appear, and how much of each you can see, depends on the call's configuration at the moment you submitted (see Workflow configuration). Steps that were not part of your proposal's sequence are omitted from the stepper, so two proposals in the same call can show different steps if the call was reconfigured between their submissions.

    When you hear the decision

    Some calls announce every allocation decision of a round together. On such a call your proposal stays In review until the call manager publishes the round's results, even if the decision has already been taken. The tracker shows the decision as the current step — named Decision when the call keeps the step internal — with Your proposal is being reviewed. beneath it. Then every applicant of the round is notified at the same time, and the proposal moves on: to Responding to an award, to Accepted, or to Rejected.

    Technical-assessment feedback

    Some steps can be made visible to applicants by the call manager. When the Technical assessment step is shared, its contributions section shows the assessment of each service provider. On a call that assesses each requested partition separately, there is one card per partition, filled in by the team of the provider that runs it, with its outcome — Accepted, Conditionally accepted with the conditions, or Rejected — and the provider's comment. A partition that rejected your proposal does not necessarily end it: some calls carry on to the decision with the other partitions.

    Note

    You only see this section when the call manager has marked the step applicant-visible. On calls that keep technical assessments internal, the section does not appear — this is expected, not an error. Whether you see who wrote an assessment depends on the call, and per-partition outcomes stay hidden while the call holds decisions for the round's publication.

    Responding to the reviews

    Some calls ask you to respond to the reviewers before the decision. The proposal then shows a step named by the call — Response to reviews, say — with Respond by date. and, above your response form, what you are responding to: the reviews and the evaluators' questions the call lists for the step. Reviews appear without the reviewers' names or their private comments.

    Fill in the response form, click Save answers, then Submit response. You are asked to confirm, as a response cannot be changed afterwards. Any of the proposal's managers can submit the response; the whole team reads it once it is submitted.

    Warning

    The response step has a deadline. If it passes without a response, the step ends as No response and the proposal moves on to the decision without it.

    Feedback to the applicant

    Calls can release a report to you, such as the panel's consolidated report. Released reports appear in the Feedback to the applicant section of the proposal, with the date they were released. What you read may be an edited version prepared for applicants. A call that announces its decisions together for the whole round releases nothing to you before the round's results are published.

    Responding to an award

    If the call includes an award response step, an approved proposal pauses for your decision before resources are provisioned. The stepper shows an Award response step. The side column — Current step and On this page — shows the decision's comment, warns The award differs from your request when it does, and ends with Accept award and Decline award. Any proposal manager of the team can answer.

    The award may differ from what you requested: the call manager can change amounts, move an item to another offering of the call, or add and remove items. The Awarded resources section on the proposal shows what was granted. An item awarded as requested carries no badge; the others are marked Changed, Moved, Added or Not awarded, and the Summary of the award totals its estimated cost. You see this section once the decision is released to you; it is read-only.

    Accept award asks you to confirm; once you do, the awarded resources are provisioned, a project is created under the call-managing organisation and your team is granted access.

    Decline award asks for a Reason for declining — it is required — and moves the proposal to Canceled. Use it when you can no longer take up the allocation.

    Tip

    Not every call uses an award response step. When it is absent, an approved proposal is provisioned automatically as soon as the decision is released to you, without asking you to confirm. On a call that allocates on a fixed date, the project starts on the round's allocation date.

    After acceptance

    An accepted proposal gains an Allocation outcome section: the project it produced and, for each requested and awarded item, the resource that came of it. On a call with steps after the award, an Extensions and final report section lets the team Request extension of the project, and holds the final report once the call team opens it towards the project's end (see After the award).
